# Business acumen — recap & real-world examples

*Part of [Business acumen for the product leader](./README.md)*

## Real-world examples

**A marketplace that reports its take rate (DoorDash).** DoorDash reports a "net revenue margin": its revenue divided by the value of orders on its marketplaces. Its 10-K put it at 13.4% for 2024. Most of each order's value passes through to merchants, couriers, taxes and tips. 🎯 *Takeaway:* order volume is not revenue. Know your take rate and what each order costs to serve. See [the language of money](./the-language-of-money.md).

**An insurer's loss ratio falls with scale and data (Lemonade).** Lemonade's 2020 10-K shows its gross loss ratio at 161% in 2017, 113% in 2018, 79% in 2019 and 71% in 2020. In its early years, claims cost more than the premium it earned. 🎯 *Takeaway:* in insurance, you learn whether your price was right only after the claims come in. See [performance and growth](./performance-and-growth.md).

**A lender's funding dries up (Upstart).** Upstart's 10-K for 2022 says that, in response to constrained loan funding, it increased the use of its own balance sheet to fund loans. A lending platform needs capital partners as much as borrowers. 🎯 *Takeaway:* in Lending, the funding side is a product dependency. See [the language of money](./the-language-of-money.md).

**Buying a bank to lower the cost of funds (SoFi).** In 2021 SoFi agreed to acquire Golden Pacific Bancorp, a community bank, to accelerate its pursuit of a national bank charter. Its own application for a new bank was already under way, with preliminary conditional approval in October 2020 from the OCC, the US national bank regulator. A charter lets a lender fund loans with deposits. 🎯 *Takeaway (general principle, not a claim from the release):* buying a licensed entity is one route to a capability. It can be faster than building that capability alone. See [portfolio strategy](./portfolio-strategy-and-allocation.md).

**A big bet reversed in about a year (Shopify).** Shopify agreed to buy Deliverr in 2022 to build its logistics business. In 2023 it agreed to sell most of the logistics business to Flexport. Shopify's results release said it would receive a 13% equity interest in Flexport, on top of its existing stake, and that Flexport would become its official logistics partner. 🎯 *Takeaway:* set stop criteria for big bets, and know your partner alternative. See [portfolio strategy](./portfolio-strategy-and-allocation.md).

**A cheap cost to serve as a strategy (Nubank).** Nubank reports monthly revenue and monthly cost to serve per active customer. In Q2 2023 it said the first passed $9 while the second stayed below $1. 🎯 *Takeaway:* a low cost to serve lets a digital bank profit from customers others cannot serve. See [monetization](./monetization-and-business-models.md).

**The partner that was never consulted (an illustration).** A fintech builds a new savings feature for six months. A week before launch, its partner bank's compliance team asks for changes to the disclosures and a new review. The launch slips a quarter. Nothing was wrong with the product. The approver was not on the org chart. 🎯 *Takeaway:* map every approval, including outside partners, before you build. See [navigating the organization](./navigating-the-organization.md). This story is invented.

**Growth that made the loss bigger (an illustration).** An insurer with a 102% combined ratio sets a premium growth target. The growth team hits it. The underwriting loss grows from $1.0 million to $1.1 million. 🎯 *Takeaway:* every growth target needs a quality guardrail inside it. See [performance and growth](./performance-and-growth.md). This story is invented.

**The through-line:** every product decision at leadership level is also a business decision. It moves revenue, cost, risk or cash, and it needs people who do not report to you. The domain decides which of those moves matter most. In Payments it is interchange and disputes. In Lending it is losses and funding. In eCommerce it is take rate and cost per order. In Insurance it is the loss ratio. In Retail it is margin and stock. In FinTech it is cost to serve, partners and rules. A leader with acumen can trace any decision to those numbers, and bring the right people along.

> **Walk-away question:** *"For my biggest bet this year: which line of the financial statements does it move, what does the case look like if the key assumption is half as good, who outside my team must agree, and what would make me stop it?"*

If you can answer all four, you are speaking the business's language. If not, re-take the <a href="assessment.html">self-assessment</a> and start with your largest gap.

## Test yourself

1. **A leader moves from eCommerce to Lending. Which kind of acumen transfers, and which must be rebuilt?**
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>General breadth transfers: statements, strategy tools, org skills. Domain depth must be rebuilt: credit losses arrive months after the decision, the price must cover the cost of funds and expected loss, and lending rules limit what data and prices you may use. (<a href="./foundations-and-assessment.md">Lesson 1</a>)</details>
2. **What is the difference between a mentor and a sponsor?**
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>A mentor gives you advice. A sponsor spends their own political capital to argue for you in rooms you are not in, such as promotion and staffing decisions. (<a href="./navigating-the-organization.md">Lesson 2</a>)</details>
3. **A launch decision has five "approvers." What is wrong, and how do you fix it?**
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>Nobody is accountable. Give each decision one accountable owner, and make the others consulted or informed. Put hard-to-reverse decisions with a senior owner. (<a href="./teams-and-collaboration.md">Lesson 3</a>)</details>
4. **A marketplace team reports $500 million of GMV as revenue. Why is that wrong?**
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>GMV is the value of goods sold through the platform. Revenue is only the share the company earns, such as its take rate and fees. Shopify's 10-K, for example, states that GMV does not represent revenue it earned. (<a href="./the-language-of-money.md">Lesson 4</a>)</details>
5. **A business case has a positive NPV of $95,000 on a $900,000 investment. Is it a good case?**
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>It depends on its sensitivity. In the lesson's example, savings only 7.6% below the estimate make NPV zero. A thin case is better staged as a pilot. (<a href="./the-language-of-money.md">Lesson 4</a>)</details>
6. **Why can a lender not learn its true default rate for rejected applicants?**
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>It never sees whether rejected people would have repaid. Data from approved loans only can mislead. Lenders use small, controlled tests near the cutoff to learn more. (<a href="./data-and-decisions.md">Lesson 5</a>)</details>
7. **Your weighted scoring puts option A at 3.85 and option B at 3.80. What do you do?**
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>Treat it as a tie. Test the weights, find the criterion the result depends on, and decide that question directly, or fund a small test of the runner-up. (<a href="./data-and-decisions.md">Lesson 5</a>)</details>
8. **Which growth-matrix cell is a renters insurer adding pet insurance for its existing customers, and what is the main risk?**
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>Product development: a new product for an existing market. The main risk is pricing with no loss history, so the loss ratio may be far off for a year or more. (<a href="./markets-and-strategy.md">Lesson 6</a>)</details>
9. **A fintech buys identity verification from a vendor. What does it still own?**
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>The regulatory responsibility. Buying moves the work, not the accountability, so it needs vendor monitoring, a fallback and audit rights. (<a href="./products-and-marketing.md">Lesson 7</a>)</details>
10. **An insurer has a combined ratio of 102%. Should it push premium growth?**
    <details><summary>Answer</summary>Not at the same ratios. Above 100%, growth makes the underwriting loss bigger. Fix the loss or expense ratio first, or grow only new business priced below 100%. (<a href="./performance-and-growth.md">Lesson 8</a>)</details>
11. **Why is a staged investment often worth more than committing everything up front?**
    <details><summary>Answer</summary>Staging buys the option to stop after the pilot. That limits the loss if the bet fails. In the lesson's example, staging raised expected value from $6 million to $9 million. (<a href="./portfolio-strategy-and-allocation.md">Lesson 9</a>)</details>
12. **What must a lender handle when it sunsets a loan product?**
    <details><summary>Answer</summary>Existing loans must still be serviced until they are repaid or sold. A sunset stops new loans, not the obligations on the old ones. (<a href="./portfolio-strategy-and-allocation.md">Lesson 9</a>)</details>
13. **Your top goal needs changes from four teams. What does the system-mirrors-the-org principle suggest?**
    <details><summary>Answer</summary>The product will keep the handoffs your structure creates. Consider grouping teams around the outcome, so one team owns most of the goal, and fund the cleanup of the old interfaces. (<a href="./leading-leaders-and-org-design.md">Lesson 10</a>)</details>
14. **A neobank's payback is 15 months. What should you stress-test first?**
    <details><summary>Answer</summary>The revenue line it depends on most, often interchange, which a rule or a partner bank controls. In the lesson's example, halving interchange pushed payback to 60 months. (<a href="./monetization-and-business-models.md">Lesson 11</a>)</details>
15. **A partner bank asks for a larger revenue share. How do you prepare?**
    <details><summary>Answer</summary>Find the interest behind the position, such as covering compliance cost. Know your best alternative, such as a second partner, and your walk-away point. Trade across issues, not only on the share. (<a href="./executive-influence-and-stakeholders.md">Lesson 12</a>)</details>

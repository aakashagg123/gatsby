Technical product management for the AI PM

The operating discipline of shipping — the role, specs, prioritization, execution, metrics, and releases that carry an idea into production.

Core insight

Product management is a loop, not a lane. AI raises the stakes: without evals and careful releases, a product is a demo with traffic.

9 lessons+ recapfor APMs & PMsdiagrams included

Product sense tells you what to build, and technical product sense tells you what the system will let you build. Technical product management is the discipline that turns those judgments into shipped software. It covers the role, the documents, the rituals and the release machinery that carry an idea from a hunch to a feature running reliably in production, and it's the part of the job you're actually judged on when the quarter ends.

For a PM moving into AI product management, the craft matters twice over. An AI feature is harder to specify, because its behaviour is probabilistic, and harder to estimate, because you discover its quality rather than design it. It's harder to launch, since a model can regress without anyone touching your code, and harder to measure, since its most interesting failures don't throw errors. Each lesson here teaches the general practice first and then shows what changes when there's a model in the build. Each one also has a diagram you can redraw on a whiteboard.

The AI capstone — eval-driven development: the eval suite is the spec, the gate, and the flywheel — bends specs, metrics, launches, and incidents
  1. The technical PM role covers what a technical PM owns, how the PM, TPM and engineering manager roles differ, and where your influence comes from.
  2. Discovery to delivery covers dual-track discovery and delivery, the four risks, and why an AI feature needs a feasibility spike.
  3. Specs, PRDs & RFCs covers the document stack, a PRD engineers respect, acceptance criteria for a feature that's sometimes wrong, and reading an RFC.
  4. Prioritization & roadmaps covers RICE, cost of delay and the Kano model, roadmaps as bets rather than promises, and how to say no.
  5. Working with engineering covers the rituals, what velocity can and can't tell you, and the trust that makes estimates honest.
  6. Metrics & experimentation covers the metric tree, instrumentation as a requirement, and A/B tests that don't fool you.
  7. Launches, rollouts & migrations covers feature flags, progressive delivery, rollback, and finishing a migration.
  8. Incidents & postmortems covers severity, incident command, honest communication, blameless learning, and the AI cases such as quality incidents and kill switches.
  9. Technical product management for AI is the capstone. It covers eval-driven development, model upgrades as migrations, the data flywheel and vibe coding.

Each lesson opens with a situation, works through one invented example step by step, and ends with things to do next. Most include short, credited moments from practitioners who have been guests on The Growth Podcast with Aakash Gupta (news.aakashg.com).
